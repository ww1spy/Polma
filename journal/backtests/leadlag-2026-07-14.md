# Lead-lag ("Nate Silver") study — cross-market time-shifted correlations (2026-07-14)

Owner asked whether one market's move predicts a related market's next move
(the way early state returns predict other states), with a pre-registered
train/test split given ~65-day API retention.

**Pre-registered design:** TRAIN = settlements May 12–Jun 12, TEST =
Jun 13–Jul 13. Universe: WTI + city high-temp ladders (3,396 settled
markets, 491 multi-strike events, hourly candles). Signal: strike A moves
≥δ in an hour while adjacent strike B is still; question: does B follow
next hour, and is following tradeable after fees (enter at ask, hold to
settlement, $10 stakes)? δ grid chosen on TRAIN only.

## Findings (all negative for trading — each informative)

1. **Bucket markets anti-correlate mechanically.** First pass grouped `-B`
   markets, which turned out to be mutually exclusive BUCKETS ("86–87°"),
   not nested thresholds. Their neighbors move OPPOSITE the mover ~70% of
   the time — probability mass conservation, not information lag. Neither
   following (−1% to −9%) nor fading (−5% to −16%) survives fees, train or
   test: the book reprices buckets faster than the spread+fee toll.
2. **True nested ladders (`-T` "above X" strikes, 1,586 markets): no
   stable lead-lag.** Follow-the-mover: TRAIN +7.32% (n=195), TEST
   **−23.44%** (n=127). Fade: TRAIN −5.98%, TEST +24.41%. A complete sign
   flip between periods is the definition of regime noise. Had we only
   looked at one window, we'd have "discovered" a +7% edge and funded it
   into a −23% period. The pre-registered wall did its job.
3. **"Executable" crossed quotes are a measurement artifact.** 6.4% of
   hourly observations show ask(K1) < bid(K2) on nested strikes (a
   riskless combo if simultaneous), best "edge" $0.98 — impossibly rich.
   Candle closes across two markets within the same hour are asynchronous;
   these were never simultaneously executable. Historical candles CANNOT
   measure arb frequency. (A live scanner checking real simultaneous books
   for ask(K1) < bid(K2) during the hourly cycle would be the honest
   instrument — cheap to add, fires rarely if ever, riskless by
   monotonicity when it truly fires. Not built; owner call.)

## Conclusion

At hourly granularity, Kalshi's intraday cross-market information
propagation is already efficient relative to its fee+spread toll. The
Silver-style edge in our system remains the event-scoped version we
already trade: a published fact (RT score) racing a lagging settlement
price — information vs. reality, not market vs. market. No rules changes.
